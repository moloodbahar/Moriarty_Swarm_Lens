Question A: what made the original batch?

This is what the original observer experiment actually asked. Fresh switched from 85% synthetic at c5 to 85% native at c6 and used later replacement claims in its explanation of the original. That is a concrete original/replacement confusion in its answer, under the stated target.

History and ledger kept 90% synthetic. Some of their wording also treats later replacement reports as facts; preserving the original answer does not mean the whole explanation is verified.

Question B: was the cleanup really completed?

That requires a separate audit: identify old and new file/commit hashes, trace the removed or overwritten rows, inspect replacement execution records, and verify which version the final analysis used.

We did not establish that synthetic rows remained, or that they were successfully removed. Nor did we establish dishonest intent. This audit gap must stay visible.

Label-swap case / Evidence and inference

A valid quote can support an invalid leap.

The label-swap run passes all 24 answer checks. Every one of its 144 evidence entries points to text that was visible at the time.

But the submitted review marks 23 of 24 answers as overclaiming about the scoring process. That is one review pass, with brief notes and no second rater.

The public score-file audit verifies identical scores for all 40 paired responses. It does not reveal whether those scores were generated separately, reused, or coordinated in one context.

The missing observation: the target's actual scoring inputs, model-call/computer-use records and execution history, tied to the file hashes.

A fictional example

The old drill and the new earthquake

An agent repeats an old earthquake drill alert. Later, a real earthquake happens. If we ask where the original alert came from, the later earthquake does not change its source.

Our test checks whether a reader keeps those two alerts separate, changes its answer when a correction really concerns the original, and admits when the evidence cannot settle the question.

04 / The pipeline

From messages to an inspectable answer.

  1. Select an authorized session; preserve message IDs, text and posting times.
  2. Write one fixed question, four explanations and six checkpoints.
  3. Run four setups on each chronological prefix.
  4. Validate probabilities, citations and saved request hashes.
  5. Review inference support; audit external artifacts separately.
  6. Compare answer trajectories and document what remains unknown.

05 / Contribution and limits

A test bench, with real failure cases.

The contribution is an auditable comparison of memory, alternative checks and source repetition on evolving swarm traces — plus concrete examples where citations pass but conclusions still need scrutiny.

One label-case episode, one repeat, one submitted review. The label case shares its May episode with the earlier original-score case. A different July outreach episode exposed explanation/category mismatches. None establishes a general accuracy gain.

We have not measured collective swarm beliefs or proven the ledger is better. The next test needs independent episodes, clearer hypotheses and independently adjudicated evidence support.
