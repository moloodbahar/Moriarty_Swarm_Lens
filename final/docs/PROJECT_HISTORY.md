# What we built and learned

1. **Source preparation and extractor.** In the earlier [AI Swarm Dynamic Hackathon repository](https://github.com/moloodbahar/AI_Swarm_Dynamic_Hackathon), we worked on chronological windows, structured extraction, exact evidence references and review. Important distinctions were posting time versus reported event time, plans versus completed actions, and original versus replacement artifacts. This did not establish a complete causal reconstruction engine.
2. **Original-score observer pilot.** A 21-message case, six checkpoints and three setups produced 18 valid outputs in the reported pilot. After later replacement updates, the fresh reader moved from .85 synthetic at c5 to .85 native at c6; history and ledger retained the original synthetic interpretation. Possible referent substitution was an exploratory interpretation, not proof of a memory benefit or original-file process truth.
3. **Target-drift follow-up.** We added a fourth setup, source recap, and manipulated the final evidence. The 36-call follow-up reused earlier trajectory steps across branches. For fresh, final synthetic probabilities were .10 with observed evidence, .80 with explicit original/replacement labels, .90 after removing direct replacement updates, and .05 after removing other context. This supports further investigation of wording and evidence selection; the variants are not independent episodes and these changes do not identify one causal mechanism.
4. **Different-topic outreach check.** A July 8 session, 80 messages, six checkpoints and four setups produced 24 validated outputs. All four final rationales acknowledged an internal template but preferred the category “preparation only,” whose definition excluded such publication. Final probabilities for that category were .75 fresh, .88 history, .93 ledger and .80 recap. This is an explanation/category mismatch under the supplied definitions, based on assistant qualitative review; it is not a population error rate or evidence that a preferred process is factually true. Possible label-meaning confusion needs testing.
5. **Final label-swap case.** We asked a different question within the same May research episode. The 98-message, 24-answer run and its 24-answer submitted review are the basis of this demo. We verified the raw saved records and separated exact score equality from uncertainty about how scores were made.

Only two distinct real episodes are represented by these three case questions. The final label case is not an independent replication of the original-score episode. The earlier historical figures are reported project records; the final label run was revalidated from its supplied raw bundle for this release. The release adds no new paid experiment.

The project moved from “extract what agents said” toward “test whether an investigator's explanation is justified.” That is the scope of the submission. It does not yet measure each swarm agent's hidden beliefs or detect collective convergence prospectively.
